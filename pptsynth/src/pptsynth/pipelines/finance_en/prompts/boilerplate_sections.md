## 2. Content Constraints

* **Faithfulness to background materials**: Use only the information contained in the document. You must not fabricate additional figures, metrics, quotes, or projections, and you must not modify or reinterpret the issuer's statements.
  * Forward-looking statements (guidance, outlook, projections): must be summarized neutrally, and must not be expanded, extrapolated, or reinterpreted.
  * Risk factors and disclaimers: may be summarized neutrally (e.g., "the issuer notes standard forward-looking risks"); do not list or elaborate unless the document itself emphasizes them.
* **Accuracy:** All content must be factually accurate, especially quantitative content. Numbers must match the source exactly, preserve their units (billions, millions, percent, basis points), and preserve their labels (GAAP vs non-GAAP, reported vs constant-currency, real vs nominal, headline vs core, baseline vs scenario). Growth rates must not be recomputed unless the document states them.
* **Brevity:** Use short, concise phrases, not long paragraphs. If you use bullet points, each slide should have no more than 6 bullet points.
* **Sufficient Depth**: Do not summarize the document in an overly superficial manner. Preserve the essential figures, segment/regional detail, and substantive narrative rather than presenting only vague conclusions.
* **Logical Flow:** The slides should present a clear narrative, with each section building naturally on the previous one — from overview through the detailed results/analysis to the outlook and closing.
* **Relevance of Information**: You must not add unrelated content.
* **Code & Markup Formatting**: Avoid raw LaTeX or Markdown code unless necessary.
* **Citation & Referencing**: Accurately reference the document's figures, tables, and exhibits.
   * If a slide uses data from the document, you must clearly indicate the source of the data on that slide (e.g., page xx, Table xx, Exhibit xx).
   * All references (if any) must be placed in the bottom-left corner of the slide.

## 3. Visual & Design

* **Images:** Include relevant images. Images must be high quality, clearly labeled, and relevant to the content.
* **Charts and Diagrams:** Use appropriate charts and tables where needed to present financial and economic data visually, rather than relying only on text.
  * If the slide includes charts or tables, ensure that all visual elements are clearly annotated (e.g., axes are labeled, units and currencies are specified, legends are included where needed, and time periods are explicit).
  * Include **figure or table descriptions** when appropriate, e.g., "The chart (from page 4 of the document) shows quarterly revenue by segment."
* **Legibility:** Use legible fonts and avoid clutter. Text should be large enough to be easily read.
* **Visual Balance:** Balance text and visuals so slides are easy to read when projected.
* **Layout:** Maintain a clean, professional layout with appropriate fonts, colors, and formatting.
* **Style Consistency**: The entire slide deck should follow a unified and coherent visual style.
* **Information Load**: Slides should avoid excessive information per page to preserve readability.

## 4. Text Quality

* All generated text should be clear, with no missing or incorrect characters or words.
* Spelling, grammar, and typography must be accurate and correct throughout the content.

## 5. Technical Fidelity Requirements

* **Quantitative Coverage**: Ensure that key quantitative data (often presented in tables or charts in the document) are included in the slide deck. The presentation should not only discuss the narrative but also present specific quantitative details (e.g., income-statement figures, segment breakdowns, growth rates, projections).
   * The slide deck must include at least 5 slides with quantitative details.

* **Numerical Exactness**: All numbers must match the document exactly, preserve their units and currency, preserve their GAAP/non-GAAP (or real/nominal, headline/core, baseline/scenario) labels, and preserve the period/region they refer to. Growth rates and percentages must not be recomputed unless explicitly stated in the document.

* **Table & Chart Traceability and Annotation**: Ensure that any tables and charts in your slide deck are consistent with the document. For every table and chart in the slides:
  * If it is directly copied from the document, clearly indicate on the slide which figure, table, or exhibit it corresponds to (e.g., page X, Table X, Exhibit X).
  * If it is newly plotted based on data from the document, clearly specify which section/page the data are taken from, and explain the meaning of each legend item and each row/column.
  * For charts, every axis, unit, currency, and time label must be explicit.

* **Point-Level Accuracy for Plots**: If line charts, bar charts, or scatter plots are used, ensure every data point exactly matches the corresponding value in the document. The values must be **precisely** the same, not merely the shape of the graph.

* **Conceptual Illustration**: The slides may include data used only for conceptual illustration. If such data are included, you must clearly indicate on the corresponding slide that the data are conceptual illustrations rather than figures reported in the document.

## 6. Presentation Tone and Audience

* **Tone:** The tone should be informative, clear, neutral, and professional. Avoid casual or promotional language, and avoid expressing personal opinions or investment judgments. Maintain a consistent tone throughout the slide deck.
* **Audience:** The intended audience matches that of the source document — for a corporate disclosure, the media, analysts, investors, and the general public; for a macroeconomic report, policymakers, economists, and informed readers. Present the content clearly and neutrally, defining specialized terms where helpful, without assuming deep specialist expertise.
